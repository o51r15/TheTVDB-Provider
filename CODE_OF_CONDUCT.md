# Code of Conduct

This is a small project with a small number of people in it. The rules are
short because they can be.

## What's expected

Be civil. Assume the person on the other end is acting in good faith, is
working with less context than you have, and is probably tired. Criticise the
code, not the person who wrote it. Accept that "no" is a complete answer to a
feature request.

Technical disagreement is welcome and useful — that's most of what a bug
tracker is for. There is a difference between "this approach breaks X" and
"this is idiotic", and the difference matters.

## What isn't

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks
- Discrimination or demeaning comments based on who someone is — age,
  disability, ethnicity, gender identity, nationality, race, religion, or
  sexual orientation
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission
- Sustained disruption — reopening settled arguments, brigading a thread,
  following someone between issues to continue a fight

## Where this applies

Issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and the wiki — anywhere
in this project's spaces. It also applies when you're representing the project
somewhere else, such as a forum thread about it.

## Reporting

Email **o51r15@sickbot.org**. Reports are read by me and not shared
further without your agreement.

Tell me what happened and where. You don't need to have a thick skin about it
first, and you don't need to be the person it happened to.

## What happens then

I'll look at it and respond. Depending on what I find, that means anything
from a private word, to editing or removing a comment, to blocking an account
from the repository. Deliberately false reports are themselves a violation.

I maintain this project in my spare time, so a response may take days rather
than hours. It will come.
